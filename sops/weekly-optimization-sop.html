Standard Operating Procedure

My Weekly Optimization SOP

The three clock cadence that keeps every account healthy after takeover, published in full because rules based optimization only counts when the rules are written down.

Takeover gets an account healthy, and this cadence keeps it that way. The system runs on three clocks. A light daily check catches anything on fire, one weekly working session does the actual optimization, and a monthly review resets the strategy. Almost every decision inside it is rules based, which means two people running this SOP on the same data should land on the same changes.

What I Check Daily

The daily pass is short by design. I watch spend pacing against the budget, look for Amazon's out of budget flag on campaigns that are making money, and check stock status on the best sellers, because a stockout on a hero product tanks conversion and distorts every metric downstream. When a best seller goes out of stock I shift spend to in stock products and shift it back on replenishment, and stock status always goes into the performance narrative so nobody misreads a supply problem as an advertising problem.

During major event windows this daily check expands into a full cadence. I monitor at fixed checkpoints, raise budgets on top performers that run out within the account cap, fix uncompetitive bids on winners showing no impressions, and pull an end of day snapshot of spend, sales, and ACoS to compare against the baseline and the prior event days. During events I also shorten the analysis window to seven days instead of thirty, because event traffic moves too fast for a monthly average to mean anything.

The Weekly Session: Sort Everything Into Three Buckets

Once a week, on the last 30 days of data, every keyword and every campaign gets sorted into one of three buckets against the target ACoS, which defaults to 20 percent when nothing better is established. A Top Performer sits at or below target with sales, so it gets pushed. A Bloated target sits above target but still sells, so it gets eased off. A Bleeder has spend with zero sales in 30 days, so it gets cut. This one sort drives the bids, the budgets, and the negations that follow.

Weekly Bid Optimization

Bid moves start from the keyword's recent cost per click and step by a fixed amount tied to its price tier. A keyword clicking above three dollars moves by 30 cents in either direction, one between two and three dollars moves by 20 cents, one between one and two dollars moves by 10 cents, one between 51 cents and a dollar rises by 5 cents or falls by 7, and one between 30 and 50 cents rises by 3 cents or falls by 5. Top Performers get raised and Bloated targets and Bleeders get lowered.

Two golden rules override everything. I never lower a Top Performer and I never raise a Bloated target or a Bleeder, so if a move would go the wrong direction the bid stays where it is. I also stay inside Amazon's suggested bid range, which means a good keyword never gets bid below the low end and a bloated one never gets bid above the high end. The rule of thumb underneath all of it is that I never underbid what sells and never overbid what does not.

Weekly Budget Management

Budgets get handled in the same session, because bids control the cost per click while budgets control how much a campaign can spend in a day. A Top Performer that is capped, meaning it carries the out of budget flag or its average time in budget sits under 100 percent, gets its daily budget raised by about 20 to 25 percent, and if it is still capped and still profitable the following week it gets raised again. A Top Performer that never hits its cap gets left alone, since more budget cannot help a campaign that is not spending what it already has. A Bloated campaign gets its bids fixed first, and only if it is still over target on the next pass does its budget come down by about 20 percent. A Bleeder drops to the minimum or gets paused outright once it has spent for 30 days with zero sales.

The golden rule here is that budget only ever rises on profitable campaigns that are actually hitting their cap and only ever falls on inefficient ones. The health gauge over the top of it is TACoS, which I aim to hold under about 30 percent per product, and a climbing TACoS usually means the bloated campaigns are carrying too much budget and need tightening first.

Weekly Harvest and Negation

The search term report gets worked weekly on a 30 to 60 day window, since search terms need a little time to collect sales. A term is a winner when it has orders, ideally two or more, and its ACoS sits at or below target. Winners get promoted into a manual campaign as exact match keywords with a starting bid near their recent cost per click, and then bid management takes over from there. The moment a term is promoted it also gets added as a negative exact in the automatic or broad campaign it came from, because otherwise the two campaigns bid against each other for the same shopper.

Negation runs in the same session. A term gets negated when it has meaningful spend with zero orders, meaning roughly five dollars or more than twice the value of a sale, when it is clearly irrelevant to the product regardless of spend, or when it was just promoted. Negative exact is the safe default, negative phrase is reserved for the rare case where a whole family of terms sharing a phrase is bad, and the negative goes into the specific campaign where the term appeared rather than account wide. The one hard exception is that a term with orders at a good ACoS never gets negated no matter how odd it looks, because sales win.

Every change from the weekly session gets logged with the date, the target, the old value, the new value, and the reason, because the log is how I see what is actually moving the account.

The Monthly Review and the Deep Clean

By the third business day of each month the prior month's actuals go in against the forecast, and a variance beyond plus or minus 10 percent triggers an investigation. The account gets re scored on current data, a score swing of two or more points forces a fresh look at the growth projection, and every standing assumption gets checked and updated. Only forward months ever change, so the original forecast stays visible and the variance tracking stays honest.

On a longer cycle the account gets a structural deep clean. Enabled campaigns with lifetime spend and zero sales get paused, campaigns running above 40 percent ACoS across the recent windows get paused, and campaigns with zero impressions get paused, with the standing exception that a catch all campaign never gets paused. Search terms that burned more than ten dollars over 90 days without converting get negated in their campaigns, and previously converting targets get consolidated into dedicated low bid campaigns so their history keeps working without the waste.

What Fires Automatically

The bucket definitions, the bid step table, and the golden rules are fully mechanical, so they fire the same way every week without judgment calls. The automation platform holds the standing ACoS thresholds and the dayparting schedule between sessions, and around events those automations get deliberately tightened before traffic surges and reset afterward, with the original settings documented first so every change can be reversed. Judgment stays reserved for where it earns its keep, meaning the monthly strategy review, the reinvestment decisions, and the escalation when a brand's goals start conflicting with their own numbers.
