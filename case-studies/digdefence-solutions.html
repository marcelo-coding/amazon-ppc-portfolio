76% Prime Event Growth for DigDefence Solutions, Then a TACoS Rebuilt from 30.79% to the Low Teens

Approximately 76% growth during the 2025 summer Prime event versus the prior year, a full account year of $1,021,922 that beat the prior year by 4.8%, and a TACoS that fell from a 30.79% baseline to 12.79% by June 2026.

DigDefence Solutions sells pest control hardware in a mature category, which presents a specific problem, because the easy growth is already captured and every additional dollar of sales tends to cost more than the last one. The brand needed to keep growing year over year in a category with a finite pool of high intent shoppers, and it needed to do that while spending more efficiently rather than less, since the client measured success in TACoS as much as in revenue. The starting point made the stakes clear, because when I benchmarked the account in September 2024 it was trending toward $70K against a $100K monthly goal while running a month to date TACoS of 30.79% against a 25% target, which meant the account was missing its sales goal and overpaying for the sales it did get.

My answer was relentless demand capture paired with new audience expansion. I ran continuous keyword extraction using research tooling and search query performance reports, feeding every relevant term into the ad plan so the account was always bidding on the freshest picture of how customers actually search. On top of that ongoing campaign optimization, I enabled AMC audiences with several active tests to reach shoppers standard targeting misses, and I launched Sponsored Brands cross promotional campaigns so that demand for one product line pulled the rest of the catalog along with it.

July 2025 closed at $121.9K in ordered product sales, up 3.4% year over year with units up 2.8%, while TACoS came in 13% lower than the same month the prior year, which means the account grew and got more efficient at the same time. The clearest proof came during the 2025 summer Prime event, where the account delivered approximately 76% growth over the prior year's event because the keyword coverage and audience work had positioned it to convert a surge in traffic that it previously would have left on the table. The full account year told the same story at a larger scale, since total sales reached $1,021,922 against $975,371 the year before for a 4.8% year over year gain.

Defending Efficiency Through a Hard Spring

The 2026 season tested the account in a different way, because a seasonal trough and a supply problem hit at the same time. February bottomed out at $34,704, and the spring months ran below the prior year while the brand worked through stockouts on key products, which the reporting called out honestly instead of burying.

"Inventory constraints are currently limiting sales potential... These stockouts have resulted in an estimated $27K in missed revenue."

April 2026 monthly summary

What mattered was that efficiency held while volume struggled, since the TACoS stayed between 10% and 16% through the whole stretch instead of drifting back toward the 30% baseline the account started from. That discipline paid off the moment inventory recovered, because June 2026 surged 59.1% month over month to $124,997 and landed within 0.3% of the prior year while spending meaningfully less to get there.

"June finished slightly below YOY, but the business delivered a more efficient month than last year, with nearly flat YoY revenue while spending $6K less in ads."

June 2026 monthly summary

"TACoS came in below target at 12.79%, showing improved spend discipline and stronger efficiency versus last year."

June 2026 monthly summary

The momentum carried into July 2026, which reached $56,580 by mid month and kept the account on its recovered trajectory.

What It Proved

A mature category account measured against a 30.79% TACoS baseline now runs in the 10 to 13% range while defending its revenue year over year, which means every dollar of ad spend works roughly twice as hard as it did when the engagement began. Efficiency that survives a stockout season is efficiency that was built into the account structure rather than borrowed from a good month.
