Vermont Smoke & Cure

Food & Snacks · Engagement data from March 2025 through June 2026

Food & Snacks

How Vermont Smoke & Cure Broke $331K in a Single Month, Then Cut TACoS to 6.60% While Sales Kept Growing

$331,629 in March 2025 ordered product sales at a 14.34% TACoS, followed a year later by June 2026 revenue of $162,372 on a 6.60% TACoS with organic sales carrying roughly 78.5% of all revenue.

Vermont Smoke & Cure, a smoked meat snacks brand, came to the month with an aggressive $330,000 sales target and a catalog that was spreading ad spend across too many products, including low margin SKUs that soaked up budget without contributing much to the bottom line. The challenge was to grow topline sales past the target while keeping advertising cost of sales in a range the business could actually live with, because growth that destroys margin is not growth worth having.

I rebuilt the advertising structure around the products that deserved the spend. I launched a comprehensive set of new Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, and Sponsored Display campaigns focused on the top selling product lines, and I reactivated and re-engaged new to brand audiences that we had originally reached through CTV campaigns so that upper funnel exposure turned into repeat purchase behavior. On the catalog side I culled low margin SKUs and added new variation packs, which concentrated spend on profitable items instead of letting it leak across the long tail, and I layered in a Back to School campaign to capture seasonal demand as it built.

March 2025 closed at $331,629 in ordered product sales, which beat the $330,000 target while coming in at a 14.34% TACoS, up 12.24% over the prior month and 13.7% over the same month the year before. The momentum held through summer, since the 2025 Summer Prime event delivered $122,898 against $94,221 in the 2024 event for a 30.4% lift, and July 2025 finished at $266,437 in revenue, up 12.3% year over year, on a 15.27% TACoS.

The Efficiency Era

The second phase of the engagement turned toward margin, because once the growth targets were secured the business wanted to know how much of that revenue it could keep. I pulled spend out of the campaigns that were paying for sales the brand would have earned anyway and let the organic flywheel that a year of focused advertising had built do more of the work. By November 2025 the account was spending $16,891 in ads to produce $37,129 in ad sales while total revenue held at $172,987 through the seasonal trough, which meant a 9.77% TACoS, and the report behind that month explains why the pullback was safe.

"Organic Sales held extremely strong: Only a 1.65% MoM decline, even as ad sales fell sharply. Organic now contributes ~78.5% of all revenue."

Monthly PPC performance report, November 2025

The efficiency kept compounding into the new year. January 2026 closed at $178,000 in revenue on a 6.18% TACoS, and the account held single digit efficiency through the spring with May at $147,954 and an 8.12% TACoS before June 2026 finished at $162,372, up 13.3% month over month, on a 6.60% TACoS.

"TACoS held at an excellent 6.18%, confirming ads are supporting revenue, not driving unnecessary cost."

January 2026 performance report

What It Proved

The account entered its second spring profitable and stayed that way, which is the outcome the whole engagement was built to produce, because a brand that only grows when the ad budget grows has rented its revenue rather than earned it.

"VT Smoke has been profitable for the past three months. They are very happy with the account's performance, particularly considering the substantial reduction in ad spend."

Account review, April 2026

The clearest stress test came at Prime Day 2026, when the account competed in the highest traffic window of the year on a fraction of its former budget and still delivered a 13.3% month over month lift for June.

"We had only 20% of last year's ad budget, but the team used it efficiently and delivered strong results relative to the spend."

June 2026 monthly summary, on Prime Day

Sustained growth at a shrinking TACoS is exactly what a focused campaign structure is supposed to produce, and this account produced it for over a year.
