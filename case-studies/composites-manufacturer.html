Composites Manufacturer

Industrial & Manufacturing

Cutting ACOS from 52% to 39% in One Week for a Composites Manufacturer

Within one week, comparing the same weekdays against the prior week, ACOS fell from 52% to 39% while daily orders held steady, so a quarter of the ad cost came out and none of the volume went with it.

This is an anonymous case study, so I will describe the client as exactly what they are: a composites manufacturer selling a technical product line on Amazon. When I got into the account, advertising ACOS sat at 52%, the campaign structure had sprawled far beyond what the catalog needed, and spend was leaking through dozens of low-signal targets that generated clicks without ever generating a clear decision. The complication was that order volume was modest, so every wasted dollar showed up immediately in the efficiency math instead of hiding inside a large sales base.

I have spent ten years in Amazon e-commerce and I run 15 to 20 accounts concurrently, so I have seen this shape of account many times: a technical product, a niche audience, a thin daily order count, and an ad console that punishes broad experimentation. On an account like this you cannot buy your way to efficiency with more spend, because the demand pool is not deep enough to absorb it, so the leverage has to come from removing waste rather than adding volume.

I made every change in one consolidation pass, because scattering edits across several weeks makes it impossible to read cause and effect on a low-volume account. First I optimized the daily budgets across every campaign, so each budget matched what the campaign had actually earned the right to spend. Then I quarantined the overspenders: I paused them and renamed them Bloated, because a rename keeps the change visible in the console and keeps every decision reversible, and I never delete anything I might later need to explain or restore.

Next I went through the account target by target. I cut bids 30% on every target sitting below 2 ROAS with no sales in the last 30 days, because those targets had already used a full month of data to prove they could spend without selling. I also trimmed the targets that converted but converted expensively, since a sale that costs more than it returns is still a leak, and it simply leaks more politely. Finally I set the winning target bids to a uniform $1.00, so the efficient targets kept their delivery while everything around them shrank.

The Same Weekdays, One Week Apart

I measured the result the honest way, comparing the same weekdays against the prior week, because a low-volume account will happily hand you a fake win if you let a strong weekday stand in for a weak one. Within one week ACOS fell from 52% to 39%, and daily orders held steady at the same level, which means the account lost none of its volume while a quarter of its ad cost came out.

The comparison holds the same weekdays against the prior week, so the drop in ACOS reflects the changes I made and not the shape of the calendar.

Efficiency creates room, and the follow-through is where that room gets spent. I raised top-of-search placement 25% on the surviving winners, because targets that convert efficiently deserve the most valuable real estate on the page, and I reallocated budget to those same winners so the recovered spend flowed back into what was working. Then I ran a year-to-date hygiene pass across the whole account: I set bids to $1 on everything holding 2+ ROAS, and I cut the year-long non-converters by 25% so they would stop accumulating quiet losses in the background.

The lesson generalizes to any low-volume technical account. Efficiency on these accounts is won by subtraction, because the winners are usually already sitting in the account and the real problem is everything crowding around them. Renaming instead of deleting matters more than it looks, because it keeps every decision auditable and reversible: the client can open the console, see exactly what I paused and why, and any call I made can be undone in one click if the data ever argues back.

I have managed over $10M in Amazon ad spend, and my flagship accounts run under 15% TACoS, but the accounts that sharpen me most are small technical ones like this, because they punish sloppy structure instantly. If your ACOS looks the way this account's 52% did, the fix is probably not a new campaign, it is a disciplined week of subtraction, and the orders you are afraid of losing were mostly never coming from the spend you would cut.
