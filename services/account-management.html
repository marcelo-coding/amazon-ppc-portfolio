Amazon Account Management

I run the whole account rather than just the ads, so catalog health, listing quality, inventory aware ad pacing, and the forecast all live under one owner who reports against the plan every week.

What the Engagement Includes

Account management with me means one person owns the whole picture, because advertising results are only as good as the catalog underneath them. I keep the catalog healthy by watching for suppressed listings, broken variations, and buy box problems before they turn into lost weeks, and I hold listing quality to the same standard I hold campaigns, since a keyword can only convert on a page whose title, images, and content actually earn the click it paid for. Ad pacing runs inventory aware, which means spend comes down before a product strands its momentum in a stockout and ramps back the moment inventory recovers, because pushing traffic at a listing that cannot ship is an expensive way to fund Amazon.

The plan itself starts with the numbers. I set the starting budget at your last 30 days of spend plus 10 to 20 percent, I score the account on ad spend, CPC, conversion rate, and organic sales share, and I build a 90 day revenue forecast whose key assumptions act as the contract between the plan and reality. From there the account settles into a weekly reporting cadence, so every week you see where performance stands against the forecast while the weekly optimization work runs underneath it, and by the third business day of each month you get the prior month's actuals with any variance beyond ten percent investigated rather than explained away. When event windows like Prime Day arrive, dedicated playbooks take over with fixed checkpoints and end of day snapshots, because event traffic moves too fast for a monthly average to mean anything.

Who It Fits

This engagement fits brands that want one accountable owner for the account instead of an ads person, a catalog person, and a reporting person who never talk to each other. It fits founders who are tired of discovering a stockout from the sales graph, and it fits brands whose growth plan has never been written down as a forecast that someone is measured against. If nobody on your team can say what the account should do next quarter and why, this is the engagement that answers it.

How It Connects to the Process

Account management is the frame around the six step process I run on every account. The engagement opens with the budget and guardrails conversation and the scoring work from my account takeover SOP, and the ongoing rhythm follows my weekly optimization SOP, with product level profitability managed in margin dollars per unit so losing products and neglected winners both get flagged. The results this discipline produces are on the case studies page, and the fastest way to see what it would do for your account is a live look at it together.
