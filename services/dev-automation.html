Services

Dev and Automation

I build the software that runs my accounts, from custom extensions and bid engines to Amazon Ads MCP tooling and AI workflows, so the daily bid changes fire as code with tiered rules that always act on complete, current numbers.

What the Engagement Includes

This engagement turns the repetitive daily work in your ad account into code that runs on a schedule and leaves a paper trail. The core is my open source SKAG optimization engine, which reads performance on a fifteen minute trigger, cuts bleeders on tiered click thresholds, and anchors every new bid to the CPC the auction actually charged rather than to the bid someone once guessed. Feeding it is my data extraction layer, which pulls the advertising reports on a schedule and normalizes them, so the rules always fire against complete and current numbers instead of a stale export. Around both sits a fleet of Amazon Ads MCP servers, one per seller account, that lets AI agents query campaigns, pull reports, and push changes through the official Ads API under my review, and where the API or the console falls short I build custom extensions that put the missing workflow directly into the tools my team already uses. The whole stack ships like production software, since the engine runs a pinned deployment where every rule change is a version that can be promoted or rolled back deliberately.

What you receive is not a tool subscription but an operated system, because I configure the rules to your target ACoS and margin guardrails, I monitor the health log of every run, and I keep judgment where it earns its keep, meaning the monthly strategy review and the reinvestment decisions rather than the four hundredth bid change of the week.

Who It Fits

Dev and automation fits brands whose accounts have grown past what any human can review by hand, which in practice means catalogs with hundreds of active targets or agencies drowning in repetitive optimization work. It also fits engineering minded founders who want their ad spend governed by policies with an author, a history, and a diff, because a bid change made by hand is an opinion that evaporates while a bid change made by code is accountable forever. If your current optimization depends on whoever happens to open the console that day, this is the fix.

How It Connects to the Process

Dev and automation is what makes my six step process scale, because the bucket definitions, the bid step table, and the golden rules in my weekly optimization SOP are fully mechanical and fire the same way every week without judgment calls. The structure that makes the rules trustworthy is explained in my essay on single keyword campaigns, and the reasoning behind the rules themselves is in my essay on bids as code. If you want to see the engine run against your own account, the next step is a short call.
