Amazon Account Management

Forecasting and Reporting

A growth plan that only lives in someone’s head can never be wrong and therefore can never be trusted, so every account I run gets a written forecast, a scored baseline, and a monthly reconciliation on a fixed calendar.

The 90 Day Forecast

Every engagement opens with a 90 day revenue forecast whose key assumptions act as the contract between the plan and reality, because a growth plan that only lives in someone's head can never be wrong and therefore can never be trusted. The starting budget gets set at your last 30 days of spend plus 10 to 20 percent, which keeps the opening period honest by anchoring it to what the account actually did rather than to what a pitch deck promised. Writing the assumptions down is the point, since a forecast with named assumptions tells you exactly which belief broke when the numbers miss.

Scoring the Account

Before the forecast means anything, the account gets scored on ad spend, CPC, conversion rate, and organic sales share, because those four numbers together describe how the business actually makes money on Amazon. The score becomes the baseline that every later report gets measured against, so progress stops being a feeling and becomes a delta. It also exposes the accounts whose healthy looking TACoS is really a shrinking ad program coasting on old organic rank, which is a problem that a topline revenue chart will hide for months.

Actuals by the Third Business Day

By the third business day of each month you get the prior month's actuals against forecast, and any variance beyond ten percent gets investigated rather than explained away, because a report that says everything is amazing on every single call is what actually creates distrust. The investigation names the assumption that broke, the decision that follows from it, and the change to next month's plan, so the forecast stays a living document instead of a document that gets quietly rewritten to match whatever happened. Underneath the monthly close, the weekly reporting cadence keeps score in real time, which means the monthly variance conversation is a confirmation rather than a surprise.

What This Produces

The outcome is an account where everyone can answer what the business should do next quarter and why, because the plan is written down, scored, and reconciled on a fixed calendar. Founders stop discovering problems from the sales graph, finance stops treating Amazon as a black box, and the agency relationship stops running on vibes, since every month either confirms the plan or produces a named reason it missed.
