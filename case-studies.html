Food & Snacks

How Vermont Smoke & Cure Broke $331K in a Single Month at a 14.34% TACoS

$331,629 in March 2025 ordered product sales, up 12.24% month over month and 13.7% year over year, followed by a 30.4% Prime event lift over the prior year.

Vermont Smoke & Cure, a smoked meat snacks brand, came to the month with an aggressive $330,000 sales target and a catalog that was spreading ad spend across too many products, including low margin SKUs that soaked up budget without contributing much to the bottom line. The challenge was to grow topline sales past the target while keeping advertising cost of sales in a range the business could actually live with, because growth that destroys margin is not growth worth having.

I rebuilt the advertising structure around the products that deserved the spend. I launched a comprehensive set of new Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, and Sponsored Display campaigns focused on the top selling product lines, and I reactivated and re-engaged new to brand audiences that we had originally reached through CTV campaigns so that upper funnel exposure turned into repeat purchase behavior. On the catalog side I culled low margin SKUs and added new variation packs, which concentrated spend on profitable items instead of letting it leak across the long tail, and I layered in a Back to School campaign to capture seasonal demand as it built.

March 2025 closed at $331,629 in ordered product sales, which beat the $330,000 target while coming in at a 14.34% TACoS, up 12.24% over the prior month and 13.7% over the same month the year before. The momentum held through summer, since the 2025 Summer Prime event delivered $122,898 against $94,221 in the 2024 event for a 30.4% lift, and by mid July the account was still trending 23.9% ahead month over month and 39.4% ahead year over year at a 14.29% TACoS. Sustained double digit growth at a stable TACoS is exactly what a focused campaign structure is supposed to produce.

Home & Garden

76% Prime Event Growth for DigDefence Solutions While Cutting TACoS by 13%

Approximately 76% growth during the 2025 summer Prime event versus the prior year, with July 2025 sales of $121.9K up 3.4% year over year and TACoS reduced by 13% against the same month the prior year.

DigDefence Solutions sells pest control hardware in a mature category, which presents a specific problem, because the easy growth is already captured and every additional dollar of sales tends to cost more than the last one. The brand needed to keep growing year over year in a category with a finite pool of high intent shoppers, and it needed to do that while spending more efficiently rather than less, since the client measured success in TACoS as much as in revenue.

My answer was relentless demand capture paired with new audience expansion. I ran continuous keyword extraction using research tooling and search query performance reports, feeding every relevant term into the ad plan so the account was always bidding on the freshest picture of how customers actually search. On top of that ongoing campaign optimization, I enabled AMC audiences with several active tests to reach shoppers standard targeting misses, and I launched Sponsored Brands cross promotional campaigns so that demand for one product line pulled the rest of the catalog along with it.

July 2025 closed at $121.9K in ordered product sales, up 3.4% year over year with units up 2.8%, while TACoS came in 13% lower than the same month the prior year, which means the account grew and got more efficient at the same time. The clearest proof came during the 2025 summer Prime event, where the account delivered approximately 76% growth over the prior year's event because the keyword coverage and audience work had positioned it to convert a surge in traffic that it previously would have left on the table.

Pet Care

A 4.36 DSP ROAS and a TACoS Drop from Roughly 16% to 12.3% for Alpha Tech Pet

February 2025 DSP delivered $44,973 in attributed revenue on $10,318 of spend for a 4.36 total ROAS, and March 2025 closed at $279,644.25, up 10.4% month over month.

Alpha Tech Pet, a pet care disinfectant brand, had a profitability problem hiding inside a growth story. Sales were moving in the right direction, but TACoS was sitting around 15 to 16%, which was heavier than the client's contribution margin targets could support, and a meaningful share of the sponsored products budget was going to spend that produced nothing. The challenge was to bring the ad cost down without starving the sales momentum that the account had worked hard to build.

I treated the ad mix as one system rather than two separate channels. I ran the DSP program with active budget rebalancing against the client's profitability targets, moving money toward the audiences and placements that were actually returning revenue, and I planned the split between sponsored products and DSP deliberately so that both contribution margin and sales growth were protected instead of trading one for the other. At the same time I gradually cut the wasted sponsored products spend, trimming it in stages so the account never lost auction presence on the terms that mattered.

The DSP side proved itself first, with January 2025 posting a 3.05 total ROAS and February climbing to 4.36 on $44,973 in DSP attributed revenue against $10,318 of spend. March 2025 then closed at $279,644.25 in ordered product sales, up 10.4% over February and 3.3% over the prior year, while the wasted spend cuts brought TACoS from roughly 15 to 16% down to 12.3% month to date by late March. The account entered April still accelerating, since the April 1 to 3 pace ran 17.47% ahead of the same days in March, which showed that efficiency and growth were compounding together rather than competing.
